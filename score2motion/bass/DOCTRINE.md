# FINGERING DOCTRINE — electric bass (4-string EADG)

Distilled real-world rules for choosing FINGER / STRING / FRET, for the automatic
fingering planner. Sources: StudyBass, TalkingBass, TalkBass forum consensus,
BassBuzz, Simandl double-bass method (via BassBros / Free Bass Transcriptions /
Double Bass HQ), No Treble, Berklee Online, Guitar World, Yamaha Hub, and the
academic tablature-generation literature (Sayegh et al.).

Fret-hand fingers: 1 = index, 2 = middle, 3 = ring, 4 = pinky.
"Position N" = fret under finger 1; one-finger-per-fret (OFPF) covers N..N+3.

## 1. Position selection (shift vs stretch vs open strings)

1.1 A position is defined by the index finger: the position number is the fret
under finger 1, and the default hand covers a 4-fret box (frets N..N+3, OFPF).
The planner's index-anchor model matches this exactly. (StudyBass "Fretting";
TalkBass "How do you define a position".)

1.2 Below roughly fret 5, prefer Simandl 1-2-4 spacing: fingers 1, 2, 4 cover
only 3 frets (N, N+1, N+2), with 3 glued to 4 as support. Fret spacing is
widest there and OFPF strains the hand. (Simandl method; BassBros; TalkBass
consensus: "1-2-4 on the first five frets, OFPF above".)

1.3 From about fret 5-7 upward, switch to OFPF (pattern A, N..N+3); frets 5-7
are a transition zone decided by the material — use OFPF there if it removes a
mid-phrase shift, else 1-2-4. (TalkBass "Fingering 1-2-4 or Finger Per Fret";
BassBuzz "Fretting Technique Showdown".)

1.4 Never stretch two adjacent fingers across more than one fret; if a note is
one fret outside the box, shift the whole hand (arm movement) instead of
stretching fingers. Extended stretch patterns (planner pattern B: N, N+2, N+3,
N+4) are legal only above ~fret 7 and only to avoid a shift inside a fast
phrase. (StudyBass: "never use two adjacent fingers to stretch over more than
one fret ... shift your hand over a fret".)

1.5 Pivot instead of shifting for a lone out-of-box note ±1 fret: thumb stays
put, hand rocks to reach it, then returns. Use when exactly one note leaves
the box and the line comes back; cost ≈ half a shift but adds pitch/accuracy
risk. (No Treble "Left Hand Technique: Pivoting"; Double Bass HQ.)

1.6 Choose the position that covers the most notes of the current phrase
without shifting; if several qualify, prefer the lower position (fatter tone,
more open-string options) unless tone or upcoming phrases argue otherwise.
(TalkBass economy-of-hand-movement consensus; Yamaha "bass fingering 101".)

1.7 Use an open string as a "free" note to cover a position shift: while the
open string rings, the hand relocates with zero time pressure. This is the
canonical jazz/walking-bass shift trick. (TalkBass "open string vs 5th fret":
"playing open strings in a walking bassline is a convenient way to make a
position shift without interrupting the flow".)

1.8 Shift on musical seams: prefer shifting on beat boundaries, after long
notes, on rests, or on open strings — never mid-way through a fast slurred
group. If a fast run cannot avoid a shift, place the shift where a string
crossing already happens. (TalkBass shifting threads; Simandl pedagogy.)

## 2. String choice (same pitch, multiple locations)

2.1 Tone rule: the same pitch on a thicker (lower) string, higher fret, sounds
fatter/rounder (more fundamental); on a thinner string, lower fret or open, it
sounds brighter/clearer with more overtones. Default for supportive bass
parts: prefer the thicker-string location when reachable. (TalkBass "open
string vs 5th fret"; Yamaha Hub.)

2.2 Economy rule overrides tone at speed: for quick up-tempo patterns pick the
location that minimizes hand travel to the surrounding notes, even if the tone
is slightly brighter/thinner. (TalkBass: economy of hand movement is factor #1
for quick patterns, tone #2.)

2.3 Keep a phrase on as few strings as practical, but prefer crossing to an
adjacent string over shifting more than ~2 frets: a one-string cross is
cheaper than a position shift. Crossing two strings at once (e.g. E→D) costs
more than adjacent crossing. (Sayegh cost paradigm; general pedagogy.)

2.4 Open strings (E A D G) are free of left-hand cost — use them for economy
and for shift cover (rule 1.7) — but they cannot be vibrato'd, are hard to
mute, and sound brighter than fretted equivalents; avoid them when the
surrounding line is fretted-and-muted funk or when consistent timbre matters.
(TalkBass "open or closed notes": some players use them whenever possible,
others only when necessary — make it a style/mode flag.)

2.5 Slap roles: thumb slaps live on the two deepest strings (E, A); pops live
on the two highest (D, G). When a slap line's pitch is playable on A or D,
route slapped notes to E/A and popped notes to D/G — the technique, not tone
economy, dictates the string. (Guitar World "How to play slap bass"; Berklee
Online; Wikipedia "Slapping (music)".)

2.6 Slap octaves are the canonical two-string-skip shape: slap root on E or A,
pop the octave two strings up and two frets higher (E→D or A→G). A→G is easier
than E→D; prefer positioning slap-octave riffs so the root sits on A when the
pitch allows. (Berklee Online; TalkingBass "How To Play Slap Bass".)

2.7 Fifth-fret equivalences (E5=A0, A5=D0, D5=G0) are the standard alternates;
when transcribing, test both and keep whichever keeps the whole riff in one
box. (TalkBass; Yamaha Hub.)

## 3. Finger choice within a position

3.1 OFPF mapping is mechanical: in position N, fret N→finger 1, N+1→2, N+2→3,
N+3→4. In Simandl 1-2-4 mode: fret N→1, N+1→2, N+2→4 (3 supports 4 and never
frets alone in low positions). (StudyBass; Simandl method.)

3.2 Guide finger on shifts: keep one finger in light contact with the string
and slide it to the new position — classical doctrine shifts UP the neck
arriving on finger 4 and DOWN the neck arriving on finger 1; the finger that
will play the target note leads the shift. (Simandl editions via TalkBass
"Simandl shifting" threads; String Bass Online "Shifting".)

3.3 After a shift, re-establish the index anchor immediately; keep the thumb
behind fingers 1-2 on the back of the neck, moving with the hand (no fixed
thumb except during a pivot). (StudyBass: "keep your thumb behind your 1st and
2nd fingers"; No Treble pivoting.)

3.4 Chromatic 4-note runs use 1-2-3-4 ascending in one position, then shift
(guide on 1) and repeat; descending runs use 4-3-2-1. In low positions the
chromatic run is 1-2-4 + shift per Simandl. (Standard chromatic exercise
doctrine, StudyBass / Hal Leonard Bass Method.)

3.5 Fast same-fret repeated notes or hammer/pull pairs prefer strong finger
pairs 1-2 and 1-3 over anything involving 4; the pinky frets but rarely leads
fast articulation. Do not avoid the pinky for normal fretting — that is a
beginner defect — but weight it as the weakest digit in ties. (BassBuzz;
TalkBass technique threads.)

3.6 Barring/rolling: the same finger may fret the same fret on two adjacent
strings by rolling the fingertip/joint (joint barring) — cheapest possible
"string cross" for perfect-fourth intervals at the same fret; use finger 1
by default. (TalkBass "Techniques for a more efficient fretting hand".)

3.7 Idle fingers hover just above the strings, curved; a finger placed for a
low note can stay down while higher fingers act on the same string (leave
fingers down behind the sounding finger — free support, faster returns).
(StudyBass; TalkingBass fretting-hand tip.)

3.8 Octave/fifth double-stop shapes: fifth (adjacent string, +2 frets) = 1 and
3 (or 1 and 4 in Simandl mode); octave (2 strings up, +2 frets) = 1 and 4
(3 acceptable with big hands). These shapes are held as one rigid hand shape,
not planned per-note. (Steven Fox box-shape lesson; standard pedagogy.)

## 4. Phrase-level planning

4.1 Plan by phrase, not by note: pick the position for the whole phrase/riff
(look ahead to the phrase boundary — typically one bar or one riff repetition,
~4-8 notes), then assign fingers inside it. Players read/plan roughly a bar
ahead. (Sight-reading pedagogy; economy-fretting doctrine, YourGuitarSage /
TalkingBass.)

4.2 Anchor on roots: the phrase's position is normally chosen so the chord
root falls under finger 1 (major-context patterns) or finger 2/4 (minor/scale
patterns), because the fill notes then sit inside the box. Root under index or
middle finger is the default box orientation. (StudyBass box patterns; Ed
Friedland "Building Walking Bass Lines" root-fifth framework.)

4.3 A repeating riff gets ONE hand shape: assign fingers once and loop; never
re-finger successive repetitions differently. If the riff transposes (e.g.
12-bar blues), translate the identical shape to the new root — same fingers,
new position. (Economy fretting: "identify what's shared between riffs";
universal groove pedagogy.)

4.4 Sequence/run planning: for a run longer than one box, pre-plan shift
points at scale-degree boundaries or open strings so each segment is a clean
box; avoid shifting on the last note before a downbeat target — arrive at the
target position one note early. (Simandl-derived pedagogy; TalkBass shifting
guidelines.)

4.5 Chord-change look-ahead: end each bar with a finger/position that lets the
next bar's root be reached with ≤1 shift — walking lines deliberately choose
approach-note locations (chromatic from above/below) that are adjacent to the
target root's position. (Ed Friedland walking-bass method.)

4.6 Showmanship override: visible big shifts, slides along one string, and
whole-neck runs are legitimate performance choices that deliberately violate
economy — model as a mode flag that inverts the shift-distance penalty (long
one-string slides preferred) for flair passages; default mode stays economical.
(Performance-practice consensus; slides on one string vs across strings is a
tone AND showmanship choice.)

4.7 Slap grooves stay in one box: classic slap lines (root-octave-ghost) are
designed to be played without shifting — dead/ghost notes are fretted by laying
fingers flat wherever the hand already is, costing nothing. Give ghost/muted
notes zero position constraint. (TalkingBass / Berklee slap lessons: muting
and ghosts happen with the resting hand shape.)

## 5. MIDI/score → fingering (transcription & auto-TAB heuristics)

5.1 Model as a shortest-path problem (Sayegh's Optimum Path Paradigm): nodes =
(string, fret, finger) candidates per note, edges = transition costs, solve
with dynamic programming/Viterbi. This is the standard benchmark for tablature
inference and matches the planner's architecture. (Sayegh 1989; arXiv
2408.05024 "MIDI-to-Tab"; UVic "Path Difference Learning".)

5.2 Enumerate all playable locations per pitch first: on EADG with 20+ frets,
most pitches have 2-4 locations; pitches below A1 exist only on the E string
(forced). Prune candidates beyond fret ~15 unless the melody forces them.
(TuxGuitar/MuseScore MIDI import behavior; general tab practice.)

5.3 Naive auto-TAB tools (MuseScore MIDI import, TuxGuitar) default to
lowest-fret / lowest-position assignment and are "usually wrong" for real
hands — the fix is precisely phrase-level cost minimization, not per-note
greediness. Do not assign notes greedily. (MuseScore issue "Finger-accurate
MIDI Guitar Tablature"; TuxGuitar docs.)

5.4 Window the DP by phrase: costs within a phrase (between rests / bar lines
/ sustained notes) matter; transitions across a rest or long note are cheap
because the hand relocates during dead time. Reset/discount shift cost across
any gap ≥ a beat. (Follows from rule 1.7/1.8; standard in fingering DP
implementations.)

5.5 Techniques constrain candidates before costing: slap→string ∈ {E,A},
pop→string ∈ {D,G}, hammer-on/pull-off pairs must share a string, slides must
share a string, ghost notes have free (string, fret ≈ current hand) placement.
Apply as hard filters, then run the cost DP on what remains. (arXiv 2510.02597
"TART: technique-aware audio-to-tab"; slap sources above.)

5.6 Learned models (masked LM, encoder-decoder) now beat hand-tuned DP on
guitar tab datasets, but their learned preferences reproduce the same rules:
small fret movement, few string crossings, open-string reuse, box locality —
validating the cost weights below. (arXiv 2408.05024, 2506.14223, 2510.10619.)

## Cost function sketch (per note-to-note transition, minimize total)

State: (string s, fret f, finger g, position P = fret under finger 1, mode ∈
{OFPF-A, stretch-B, Simandl-124}). Suggested weights, normalized so a plain
1-fret shift = 1.0:

- SHIFT: |ΔP| ≥ 1 → cost = 1.0 + 0.5·(|ΔP| − 1)  (superlinear kink: any shift
  hurts; extra frets hurt less than the first). ×2.0 if the available time to
  the next note < ~120 ms; ×0.25 if it occurs across a rest/long note; ×0.4 if
  the previous note is an open string (free relocation, rule 1.7); −30% if a
  guide finger can slide (same finger, same string).
- PIVOT: |ΔP| = 1 for a single note returning to the box → cost 0.5 instead of
  two shifts (rule 1.5).
- STRING CROSS: adjacent string 0.25; two-string skip 0.6; three-string skip
  1.0. Waive the adjacent cost when joint-barring (same fret, same finger,
  adjacent string, rule 3.6).
- STRETCH (mode B): entering the 5-fret stretch pattern 0.8, plus 0.3 per note
  played with the stretched spacing; forbid below fret 7; forbid entirely in
  Simandl zone (frets 1-4). (Rules 1.2-1.4.)
- OPEN STRING: bonus −0.3 (economy) in default mode; penalty +0.4 in
  "consistent-timbre/funk-mute" mode. Always bonus −0.6 when the open note is
  immediately followed by a shift. (Rules 2.4, 1.7.)
- FINGER STRENGTH: per-note micro-costs 1:0.0, 2:0.0, 3:0.1, 4:0.15; double
  finger-4 cost for notes shorter than ~100 ms or leading a fast group (rule
  3.5). Same-finger consecutive different-fret on same string (crawling) +0.5
  unless it is a slide/guide.
- POSITION STABILITY: −0.2 per note that keeps P unchanged while a riff loop
  is active (detected repetition ⇒ lock the shape, rule 4.3); +0.5 penalty for
  re-fingering a bar that is a repeat of an already-fingered bar.
- TONE PRIOR (tie-breaker only, weight 0.1): prefer thicker string / higher
  fret for sustained supportive notes; prefer lower position overall (rule
  1.6, 2.1).
- ROOT ANCHOR: −0.3 when a chord root lands on finger 1 (or 2) at a phrase
  start (rule 4.2).
- TECHNIQUE FILTERS (hard, pre-DP): slap ⇒ s ∈ {E,A}; pop ⇒ s ∈ {D,G};
  slap-octave pairs ⇒ (s, s+2 strings, +2 frets) shape, prefer A→G over E→D;
  hammer/pull/slide pairs ⇒ same string; ghost notes ⇒ inherit current P free.
- FLAIR MODE: multiply SHIFT weight by −0.3…0 for designated showmanship
  spans and add a −0.4 bonus for same-string slides ≥ 3 frets (rule 4.6).

Solve with Viterbi over the phrase window (rule 5.4); beam width ~20 states is
ample for 4 strings × 4 fingers.
